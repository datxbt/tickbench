Liquidity Vacuum Fade

The signal is real. It is just smaller than the spread it has to cross. Measured on 283 million gold ticks, the edge is worth about thirty dollars a trade and costs a hundred and ten.

Dev −71.9%  /  out of sample −46.3%  /  do not trade

Every sensitivity variant loses, including one with slippage and commission both set to zero. The participation filter that the specification identifies as the active ingredient does not separate reverting moves from non-reverting ones.

Where the money goes

An edge of thirty dollars against a cost of a hundred and ten

Every trade is re-priced twice: once at the actual bid and ask it would have filled at, and once at the mid. The difference between those two is the entire cost of participating, and it is the number that decides this.

The active ingredient

Participation does not predict reversion

The specification's central claim is that a big move on a normal or low tick rate is a vacuum that reverts, while the same move on a high tick rate is news that does not. That is testable directly, with no trade construction in the way: take every setup, drop the participation filter, and measure how far the mid comes back.

The gradient runs the wrong way. The three buckets the strategy trades average +$0.038 at fifteen minutes; the buckets it excludes as "news" average +$0.063. The single best bucket is the highest-participation one, which the rule is specifically designed to avoid.

Neither group is distinguishable from zero. Standard errors put both at t ≈ 0.6, so the honest reading is not "the filter is backwards" but "the filter is not measuring anything". Tightening it to 0.9 or removing it entirely both still lose.

The fifth row is the one that ends the discussion. With no slippage and no commission at all — an account no broker offers — the strategy still loses 12.5%, because the remaining cost is the bid-ask spread and $57.45 of it per trade is more than the $43.62 of edge.

Tightening V to 5.0 cuts the loss to −22.5%, but only by trading 120 times instead of 900. Its edge at mid is negative. That is a smaller loss, not a better strategy.

One thing the spec got backwards

Slippage is not worse in a vacuum

The specification warns that "vacuum conditions are exactly when slippage is worst". That is measurable: take the mid 250 ms after each of the 891 entries and sign it against the position.

Entries drift very slightly in favour over the fill window, and are adverse less than half the time. The backtest charges about fifteen times the measured magnitude and the strategy still loses, so the assumption being wrong in this direction only strengthens the result.

What would have to change

What this rules out, and what it does not

  • Fading tick-vacuums on gold at this horizon does not work A median hold of 2.1 minutes puts the whole trade inside a window where the round-turn cost is comparable to the entire typical move. Stage 2 measured this independently: on the dev split a gold round turn costs 1.09 bps, and a one-minute bar's mean absolute move is 1.5 times that.
  • The participation ratio is not a usable filter It does not order setups by how much they revert, in either direction, at any threshold tested. Whatever separates a vacuum from news, quote count over a hundred ticks is not it.
  • The measurement idea is not dead Move per quote is a real quantity and the setups do revert slightly more often than chance — 53% at fifteen minutes, consistently across buckets. That is a genuine signal. It is roughly one part in twenty of what it would need to be worth trading here.
  • The economics change with the holding period, not the filter Cost is fixed per round turn, so the only lever that moves the ratio by an order of magnitude is holding longer. A version targeting hours rather than minutes would face a cost of about a tenth of the move it is trying to capture, instead of three times it. That is a different strategy, and it would need its own test.

Method

How this was run

Tick-by-tick on the raw feed, not on bars. Entries fill at the bid or ask of the tick that triggered them; a long is opened at the ask and closed at the bid. Stops are market orders and take slippage; take-profits are limits and fill at their price or not at all. Commission is $3.50 per lot per side, charged on partial closes in proportion. Size is 0.5% of running equity against the stop distance, one position at a time, with a 2% daily loss limit that blocks new entries.

Two guards matter more than the rest. Windows spanning a session gap are refused — a hundred ticks straddling a weekend show an enormous move on a near-zero tick rate, which is exactly the setup's signature and the one place it means nothing. And the EMA baselines are lagged one tick, so the tick being judged is never part of the baseline judging it.

Two ambiguities in the specification were resolved by running both ways. The prose says an extension beyond the extreme cancels the setup while the pseudocode resets its counter; the prose version is the headline and the pseudocode version is in the sensitivity table, losing more. "23:55–00:15 server time" was read as 20:55–21:15 UTC, the rollover window measured in Stage 2.
